# Future Outlook: Where Quantum AI Is Headed

## In Plain English

Closing out this repo (and the whole qoherence trail), it's worth asking plainly: what does the next five to ten years plausibly look like?

**Short term (now through ~2028):** more of what's already working — quantum-inspired optimization expanding from pilots into standard tooling in logistics, finance, and manufacturing; real quantum hardware QML staying mostly in research and small proof-of-concept pilots, constrained by noise and barren plateaus; benchmarking discipline continuing to improve, quietly deflating some earlier hype.

**Medium term (~2028-2033):** as fault-tolerant, error-corrected quantum computers (see `qoherence-core/docs/04-expert/01-fault-tolerant-computation.md`) become real rather than theoretical, expect the QML techniques already designed today (VQCs, quantum kernels, QNNs) to get a genuine capability boost — not because the algorithms will have changed much, but because the hardware finally running them reliably will have. This mirrors classical AI's own history: the 2012 deep-learning breakthrough used decades-old algorithms, unlocked by new hardware (`01-beginner/02-how-classical-ml-works-recap.md`).

**Longer term, and genuinely uncertain:** whether QML ever delivers a broad, general-purpose advantage over classical AI for everyday tasks (not just narrow, quantum-friendly ones) remains an open scientific question, not a settled certainty in either direction. The honest, defensible position — the one this repo has tried to model throughout — is neither "quantum will revolutionize AI" nor "quantum AI is pure hoax," but something more useful: a real, still-developing research field, already delivering modest, concrete value through its quantum-inspired classical half, with its quantum-hardware half's biggest potential gains tied directly to hardware progress that is real, measurable, and still years away from removing today's central obstacles (noise, barren plateaus, limited qubit counts).

## Now With the Math

No new formulas here — this closing page is deliberately about judgment, not equations, which is itself a fitting way to end: every piece of math across this repo (rotation angles, overlaps, gradients, energy landscapes) exists in service of that same judgment call — knowing what a real result can and can't yet claim.

## A Bit of History

It's worth closing on the same note `qoherence-docs/docs/ethics-and-society.md` closed on: the physicists who built quantum mechanics a century ago had no way to know it would eventually raise questions about machine learning, corporate marketing claims, or delivery-truck routes in Lisbon. Quantum AI, as a field, is barely two decades old (dating roughly from Lloyd, Mohseni, and Rebentrost's 2013 paper) — young enough that today's genuinely open questions may well be settled, one way or another, within a working reader's career. That's a good reason to keep learning about it carefully, and a good reason for this whole trail — from a century of quantum-mechanics history through to this exact sentence — to have insisted on precision over excitement the entire way.
