# Superposition & Entanglement (Beginner)

## Superposition, in plain language
Superposition isn't "the qubit is secretly 0 or 1 and we just don't know."
It's genuinely in both states at once, mathematically — this is what gives
quantum computers their power: operating on many possibilities in parallel.

## A simple analogy
Imagine flipping a coin so fast it's a blur — that blur represents "both
heads and tails" until it lands. A qubit in superposition is similar, except
the "blur" is a precise mathematical combination, not just fast flipping.

## Entanglement
When two qubits are entangled, they share a single combined state. Measuring
one instantly tells you something about the other — even though neither
qubit was "decided" beforehand. This isn't communication (nothing travels
between them), but a correlation baked into the shared state.

## Why entanglement matters
Entanglement is the resource quantum algorithms use to get advantages over
classical computers. Without entanglement, a "quantum" computer is just a
weird classical computer with extra randomness.

## Common beginner misconception
Entanglement does NOT let you send information faster than light. You can't
control what a measurement result will be, so no usable message can be sent.
