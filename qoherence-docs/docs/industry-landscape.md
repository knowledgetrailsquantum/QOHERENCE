# The Quantum Computing Industry Landscape (2025–2026)

This doc ties the technical concepts spread across all six qoherence repos back to the companies building real quantum computers today, their public roadmaps, and the milestones that actually matter versus the ones that are mostly marketing. Read this after `qoherence-core/docs` (you'll need the qubit/gate/entanglement vocabulary) and alongside `qoherence-hardware/docs` (which covers the underlying physical technologies in more technical depth).

## IBM
The longest-running large-scale industry quantum program, and the most vocal advocate for treating error mitigation and near-term "quantum-centric supercomputing" as a serious, separate track from full fault tolerance rather than just a stopgap. Superconducting-qubit hardware (transmons on a heavy-hex lattice — see `qoherence-hardware/docs/02-intermediate/01-qubit-technologies.md`). Public roadmap has moved through named processor generations (Eagle, Osprey, Condor — the last crossing 1,000+ physical qubits — then Heron, emphasizing quality over raw count) toward a stated goal of fault-tolerant systems with hundreds of logical qubits later this decade, alongside published research on quantum LDPC codes (bivariate bicycle codes) aimed at reducing error-correction physical-qubit overhead versus the surface code (see `qoherence-mitigate/docs/03-advanced/01-stabilizer-codes.md`). IBM has also been the most consistent public voice cautioning against overstated "quantum supremacy" claims from competitors, notably challenging aspects of Google's 2019 Sycamore result with improved classical simulation arguments (see `qoherence-core/docs/03-advanced/03-complexity-theory.md` and `qoherence-sim/docs/03-advanced/01-tensor-network-methods.md`).

## Google Quantum AI
Also superconducting-qubit hardware. Made the original 2019 "quantum supremacy" claim (Sycamore) and, more significantly for the field's fault-tolerance timeline, the December 2024 Willow chip result demonstrating below-threshold surface-code error scaling for the first time in a real device at meaningful scale (see `qoherence-core/docs/04-expert/01-fault-tolerant-computation.md` and `qoherence-mitigate/docs/03-advanced/01-stabilizer-codes.md`) — widely regarded as one of the most important experimental hardware milestones in the field's history, because it's the first hard evidence the core error-correction math actually works as theory predicts in a physical device, not just on paper. Google's roadmap is explicitly staged toward first demonstrating one very good logical qubit, then scaling logical qubit count.

## Microsoft
Pursues a distinctive dual strategy: (1) in-house research into **topological qubits**, using Majorana zero modes in engineered semiconductor-superconductor nanowires, aiming for hardware-level error protection that could dramatically reduce error-correction overhead if it works — the 2025 "Majorana 1" chip announcement claimed evidence of this but drew significant scientific scrutiny and debate over the strength of the evidence, illustrating how much harder this approach's basic physics validation has been versus other platforms; and (2) **Azure Quantum**, a cloud platform reselling access to multiple third-party hardware vendors (IonQ, Quantinuum, Rigetti, Pasqal, and others), plus **Azure Quantum Elements**, applying quantum-inspired and quantum-classical hybrid methods (including VQE-family algorithms, see `qoherence-algorithms/docs/03-advanced/01-vqe-and-qaoa-theory.md`) to materials science and chemistry problems for enterprise customers today, independent of whether Microsoft's own topological hardware bet succeeds.

## IonQ and Quantinuum (trapped ion)
Both use trapped-ion qubits (see `qoherence-hardware/docs/02-intermediate/01-qubit-technologies.md`), typically leading the industry on two-qubit gate fidelity (often above 99.9%) and offering effectively all-to-all qubit connectivity — a genuine architectural advantage for algorithms needing complex entanglement patterns (relevant to `qoherence-hardware/docs/03-advanced/01-connectivity-and-transpilation.md`'s discussion of routing overhead). IonQ is publicly traded and markets a proprietary "algorithmic qubits" composite benchmark metric (see `qoherence-bench/docs/02-intermediate/01-fidelity-metrics.md`). Quantinuum (formed from Honeywell's quantum hardware division plus Cambridge Quantum) has published some of the highest publicly reported fidelity numbers in the industry and has been a leading participant in demonstrated logical-qubit error-correction experiments.

## Rigetti, Amazon (Braket), and neutral-atom players (QuEra, Pasqal, Atom Computing)
Rigetti builds superconducting hardware and, notably, was an early mover in offering direct cloud access to real quantum processors. Amazon doesn't primarily build its own competitive qubit hardware for general use but operates Braket, a cloud marketplace reselling access to IonQ, Rigetti, QuEra, and other vendors' hardware alongside its own simulation tools — a strategic position similar to Microsoft Azure Quantum's multi-vendor approach, rather than a single-hardware bet. QuEra, Pasqal, and Atom Computing (the last partnered with Microsoft) build neutral-atom hardware using optical-tweezer-trapped atoms, which has shown some of the fastest recent qubit-count scaling and produced some of the largest logical-qubit-count error-correction demonstrations via Harvard/MIT/QuEra collaborations.

## PsiQuantum and photonic approaches
Pursuing photonic qubits with a deliberately fault-tolerant-first strategy (skipping an intermediate NISQ product stage entirely) and a bet on leveraging existing semiconductor photonics manufacturing infrastructure for eventual scale — a notably different strategic posture from most competitors' staged, incremental roadmaps.

## What actually distinguishes real progress from hype
When reading quantum computing news, the qoherence repos' recurring themes are the right filter to apply:
- **Qubit count alone is close to meaningless** without accompanying coherence time and gate fidelity numbers (`qoherence-core/docs/03-advanced/02-density-matrices-and-mixed-states.md`).
- **"Quantum supremacy/advantage" claims** should specify the exact task and address the best known classical simulation approach as a baseline, not just assert classical infeasibility (`qoherence-core/docs/03-advanced/03-complexity-theory.md`, `qoherence-sim/docs/03-advanced/01-tensor-network-methods.md`).
- **Logical qubit counts and demonstrated below-threshold error-correction scaling** are currently the most meaningful fault-tolerance progress indicators, and are still small (single digits to low tens of demonstrated logical qubits across the industry) relative to the thousands-plus needed for algorithms like cryptographically-relevant Shor's factoring (`qoherence-core/docs/04-expert/01-fault-tolerant-computation.md`, `qoherence-algorithms/docs/04-expert/01-implementing-shors-period-finding.md`).
- **Near-term commercial value today** flows almost entirely through NISQ-era, mitigation-supported algorithms (VQE, QAOA) applied to chemistry, materials, and optimization problems (`qoherence-algorithms/docs/03-advanced/01-vqe-and-qaoa-theory.md`, `qoherence-mitigate/docs`) — not through fault-tolerant algorithms, which remain a multi-year-to-decade engineering horizon across every major player's own public roadmap.
